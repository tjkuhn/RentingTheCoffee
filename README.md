IT Guy
So on my way out today, I ran into the IT guy who helped me when it was time to refresh my laptop (new laptop, move all the files, make sure the right apps are there, all that stuff). He said hello and asked if I remembered him and gave me the context of my laptop, "you know, the black one." I keep going back and forth over whether he was joking on this last part...I mean almost all of them are black....everywhere. Its novel when they aren't. 

We went on to talk about how fast the year was coming to a close, the nature of time in Q4, and how it was getting cold, my origins in Western NY, his friend from Canada, thinning of one's blood over time living in Southern CA, and how he's lived here his whole life and he wouldn't trade it - no matter how expensive it gets. 

And then we said good night and parted ways. I was surprised that he thought I might not remember him as it wasn't that long ago that he helped me, but I suppose that me thinking of him as the "IT Guy" and not placing his name immediately bears him out in part. I'm not sure why, but I keep thinking about him and how anonymous he seemed to feel. And actually *be* to most people. 

And I keep thinking about him. I work with the Tech people, the IT folks, the Data gearheads, whatever a lot. I have for years. If **I** think of him as the "Help Desk Guy".....well, I imagine he gets worse. Somehow, that didn't make me feel better about it, but the fact that he's just a guy, like me, trying to get through the day, get his stuff done, get home to the people and places, pets and things,  hobbies and concerns that take up his time when he's not at work....that, that made me feel a bit better. Like me.


~TJK


Adaen is Back
20261111 - Adaen of Bridgewater
Harlan was content. It was a lively night in the Tavern. Rusty was having a good-natured argument with some dwarves from the Northern Reaches about the merits of Phillips-head over flat-head screwdrivers; job-agnostic utility be damned in this case. The tall, spindly bard who claimed to be a king was performing in the corner to a small audience; a sonnet about a special pig seemed to be soothing them. The group of newcomers were desperately trying to read a stained map as they decided their bearing for the morrow. 

It was that perfect mix of joyous wonder, goodwill to mankind, and muted revolution that makes for a perfect night.  The Chronicler always warned him never to talk to the pitcher during an active no-hitter. So yes, it was a near perfect night, until he  made his entrance; then the tone changed considerably. Harlan had seen the Stranger, as most called him, before, but not for a long time. He had a name, Adaen, I think it was; and he usually came by boat…canoe more specifically. He usually came alone and he had a way about him that…..well, I’ll let you decide what his way did and didn’t do.

Let’s just say that Adaen of Bridgewater usually had something on his mind and he wasn’t always shy about sharing it. He liked to watch and take stock too and many remembered him for that, but when he was done watching he would open his mouth and sometimes what came out was hoo-hockey, but sometimes it cut to the bone. One could never quite tell in advance and sometimes even afterward. Harlan wasn’t quite sure what he might say tonight, but he was sure that he wouldn’t be wondering long….

Yes, I’m back. I’ve been coming here for a long time (my profile says 20+ years). Here and other places online. Some are as perennial as the grass and some are gone. I’d like to use this thread to say hello. Hello to new folks that don’t yet know me and Hello to old friends from times past. 
To say hello, and let you know what I’ve been thinking about and what I’ve been working on. Most of it will be gaming, of course. I’ll add little pieces now and then to this thread as responses; and of course if I’ve got something specific to talk about, I’ll set up a specific thread to do that. Good Gaming, All. Talk soon.
